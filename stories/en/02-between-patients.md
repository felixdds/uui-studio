# Five Months of Development in the Gaps

[简体中文](../02-between-patients.md) · English

When I say I developed UUI Studio over roughly five months, it is easy to picture me sitting at a computer every day, giving instructions to Codex. That is not how it happened.

I continued working as usual. I brought my computer to work and used the gaps between seeing patients to give instructions, discuss problems, and come back to check the results.

## Leave a task and come back to see what happened

For example, after administering anesthesia, I could use the time while it took effect to check Codex's progress. Before starting the next treatment, I could leave another task. Once treatment was finished, I could return, see what it had done, and decide what to change next.

Sometimes it had finished, and we could move on. Sometimes the result did not match what I had in mind, so I explained what was wrong and asked for adjustments. The project moved forward a little at a time, alongside my regular work.

Sometimes, while accompanying my wife shopping, I would connect to Codex remotely from my phone. I would not necessarily ask it to write code. We might simply discuss an architectural question: does this rule make sense? Can we reuse an existing mechanism? Which definitions could actually be derived?

Sometimes the code moved forward. Sometimes only an idea did. Both were part of the project.

## Each doing what they do best

My principle is simple:

> If AI can handle something, I try to let it. We each do what we do best, without dismissing others or discounting my own contribution.

I do not know Rust, so I let Codex handle the Rust implementation. If its design does not meet my architectural requirements, I keep asking questions and checking. I am happy to delegate execution work that AI can do. I remain involved in judgments about requirements, rules, and user experience.

That division of work does not require me to type every line of code. Nor does it mean I can say one thing and stop caring about the result. Checking whether what comes back is what I intended is something I keep doing.

## What five months means

Five months is the elapsed time over which the project progressed, not five months of full-time programming. I did not time every small interval, and I do not intend to use that duration to calculate working hours or productivity multipliers.

What interests me more is that this division of work lets me keep participating in a field of software engineering that would previously have been difficult for me to enter on my own. I work and live as usual, raise questions and make judgments when I have time, and let Codex continue with the parts it is good at.

Those small intervals gradually added up to today's UUI Studio.
